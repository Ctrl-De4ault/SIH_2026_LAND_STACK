01 Executive summary

A federated operating system for land, not another record viewer

Land Stack turns fragmented, department-owned land data into a unified, interoperable Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI). Every dataset — cadastral map, Record of Rights, registration, zoning, tax, utility — is bound to a single georeferenced land parcel identified by its ULPIN. Departments read and write through open APIs; citizens see one transparent, map-first interface.

The gap

Data lives in silos

Cadastre, RoR, registration, planning, and tax systems rarely talk to each other. Verifying one parcel means visiting many portals and offices.

The idea

Parcel as the join key

ULPIN becomes the universal foreign key. All governance data is attached to a spatial parcel, so a single query returns the whole picture.

The outcome

One trustworthy view

Faster transactions, transparent ownership, better planning, and AI-assisted governance — replicable across every State and UT.

In one line: Land Stack is to land records what UPI is to payments — a thin, standardised, interoperable layer that lets many systems and citizens transact on a common rail, without forcing every State to abandon its existing databases.

02 Problem & opportunity

Land governance is fragmented by design

Because land is a State subject, records evolved independently across departments and States — in different formats, schemas, units, languages, and workflows. The cost is borne by citizens and the State alike.

What breaks today

  • Cadastral maps (BhuNaksha) and textual records (Bhulekh) are separate, weakly linked systems.
  • Registration, mutation, planning, tax, and utilities each run their own siloed databases.
  • Ownership verification is slow, presumptive, and prone to disputes and fraud.
  • No common parcel identity means the same land is described differently in every system.
  • Citizens navigate multiple portals and offices for a single land transaction.

Why now

  • ULPIN / “Bhu-Aadhaar” gives every parcel a permanent, geo-anchored identity.
  • DILRMP has digitised large volumes of RoR and cadastral data to build upon.
  • Mature DPI patterns (Aadhaar, DigiLocker, API Setu) provide proven building blocks.
  • Cloud, PostGIS, OGC services and MapLibre make parcel-scale GIS affordable.
  • AI/ML and satellite change-detection make proactive land governance feasible.

Context: The Department of Land Resources launched Land Stack in pilot locations of Chandigarh and Tamil Nadu on 31 December 2025, with a plan to cover one city and one village in every State/UT before scaling nationwide. This PRD describes a scalable prototype for that mandate.

03 Positioning

Why Land Stack is not Bhulekh

Bhulekh (and its map sibling BhuNaksha) are State-level portals for viewing and downloading land records. They are useful, but they are single-purpose, read-oriented, State-siloed, and rarely interoperable. Land Stack is a different category of thing: a transactional, parcel-centric, cross-department platform. Below is exactly how we differ.

1

Parcel-centric DPI, not record lookup

Everything hangs off a georeferenced parcel keyed by ULPIN. One identity unifies map + record + registration + tax across systems.

Bhulekh: textual RoR lookup, map lives elsewhere (BhuNaksha), no shared key.

2

Interoperable by open APIs

Departments integrate through published REST + OGC APIs, standard schemas, and consent — a shared rail, not a walled portal.

Bhulekh: siloed per State; no standard cross-department API layer.

3

AI/ML + geospatial intelligence

Satellite change-detection, encroachment alerts, predictive dispute risk, and decision dashboards built in.

Bhulekh: static display; no analytics or change detection.

4

End-to-end citizen services & workflows

Search, verify, apply, pay, and track mutation / registration / permission / dispute — a full transaction, not a PDF.

Bhulekh: mostly view-and-download; transactions remain offline / partial.

04 Vision, goals & principles

What we are building, and the rules we build by

Vision: Every land parcel in India has one trustworthy digital identity and one transparent window into its rights, restrictions, liabilities, and services — for citizens and the State alike.

Product goals

  • Bind all land datasets to a georeferenced parcel via ULPIN.
  • Enable secure, consented, cross-department data exchange through open APIs.
  • Deliver map-first citizen services: search, verify, transact, track.
  • Provide role-based dashboards and analytics for administrators.
  • Ship a modular prototype that is configurable per State/UT and replicable nationally.

Design principles (DPI)

  • Federated, not centralised — States keep their systems of record.
  • Interoperable by default — open standards, published schemas.
  • Minimal & consent-first — least data, purpose-bound, auditable.
  • Parcel-centric — spatial identity is the organising principle.
  • Inclusive — multilingual, mobile-first, low-bandwidth friendly.

06 The three-layer model

Base → Essential → Use-case, all bound to ULPIN

Information is organised into three spatial layer categories. The base layer is the spatial truth; essential layers define rights and restrictions; use-case layers extend services. ULPIN is the common thread through all three.

Layer 01 · Base

Spatial foundation

Georeferenced cadastral maps, parcel boundaries (polygons), and the unique parcel identifier. This is the coordinate frame every other dataset attaches to.

Cadastral basemapParcel geometry (polygon)ULPINSurvey / re-survey (drone, ETS)CRS: WGS84 / UTM
Layer 02 · Essential

Rights, restrictions & liabilities

Core governance datasets linked to each parcel that together define ownership, rights, restrictions, liabilities, and permissible use.

Record of Rights (RoR)Registration / deedsMaster planBuilding permissionsEncumbrance & mortgageLand use & zoning
Layer 03 · Use-case

Extended governance & services

Additional datasets that extend the platform's governance and citizen-service capabilities, integrated as needed per context.

Utility infrastructureProperty taxationValuation referencesInfrastructure networksEnvironmental / restriction zonesService linkages

The join: a parcel is 1 : N to layer records. Given a ULPIN, the platform returns geometry + all linked essential and use-case records the requester is entitled to see — the single query Bhulekh cannot answer.

08 Key user journeys

How value shows up in practice

Citizen — verify before you buy

  1. Search a parcel by map pin, address, or ULPIN.
  2. See owner(s), area, land use, and any encumbrance/mortgage flags.
  3. Pull a consent-based, DigiLocker-verifiable ownership certificate.
  4. View dispute-risk and recent change-detection flags before deciding.

Registration → mutation, straight-through

  1. Sub-Registrar registers a deed in NGDRS, linked to the ULPIN.
  2. Land Stack auto-creates a mutation task for the Revenue office.
  3. Patwari verifies on the map + record view and approves.
  4. RoR updates; citizen is notified; audit trail is immutable.

Planner — building permission check

  1. Open the parcel; overlay master plan & zoning layers.
  2. System validates the proposal against permissible land use.
  3. Route for approvals; flag environmental/restriction zones.
  4. Issue permission linked to the parcel and visible to the citizen.

Admin — detect an encroachment

  1. Satellite change-detection flags new construction on public land.
  2. Alert lands in the admin dashboard with before/after imagery.
  3. Official opens a case; workflow assigns field verification.
  4. Outcome recorded against the parcel with full provenance.

11 State-diversity & configurability

Solving “land is a State subject”

The hardest requirement is diversity: record formats, schemas, units, field counts, language, terminology, and workflows differ by State. Land Stack absorbs this with a canonical model plus per-State configuration — never a hard-coded assumption.

Configuration surface

  • Canonical schema + adapters: each State maps its fields to the canonical parcel/RoR model via declarative config.
  • Unit normalisation: bigha, katha, acre, hectare, guntha → SI internally, displayed in local units.
  • Terminology packs: khatauni / jamabandi / patta / RTC mapped to canonical roles.
  • Language packs: UI + record transliteration per State language.
  • Workflow templates: mutation/registration steps configured, not coded.

How a new State onboards

  1. Load the State's cadastre & sample RoR into the layer catalogue.
  2. Author a mapping profile (fields, units, terms, language).
  3. Select workflow templates & role definitions.
  4. Validate with sample parcels; publish APIs; go live for a pilot city + village.

12.2 API & interoperability standards

Standards adopted

  • REST + OpenAPI 3.1 — versioned (/v1), JSON, cursor pagination.
  • OGC services — WMS, WFS, WMTS; GeoJSON & vector tiles for delivery.
  • OAuth 2.0 / OIDC — tokens; consent artefacts for institutional access.
  • Aadhaar / DigiLocker / API Setu patterns for identity & document issue.
  • Metadata — ISO 19115 / DCAT for dataset descriptions in the catalogue.

12.4 GIS standards

CRS

WGS84 / UTM

Storage in EPSG:4326; display in Web Mercator (3857); India UTM zones 42N–47N for measurement.

Services

OGC-compliant

WMS/WFS/WMTS via GeoServer; GeoJSON + vector tiles (PMTiles/MVT) to the client.

Quality

Topology

PostGIS topology rules: no gaps/overlaps; validated parcel closure & snapping.

12.5 Security framework

Controls

  • AuthN: OIDC; Aadhaar-based citizen auth (voluntary); MFA for officials.
  • AuthZ: fine-grained RBAC + attribute rules by role, State, and layer.
  • Consent: purpose-bound, time-boxed tokens for third-party reads.
  • Crypto: TLS 1.3 in transit; AES-256 at rest; signed credentials.

Assurance

  • Audit: immutable, append-only log of every access with actor + purpose.
  • Integrity: tamper-evident record hashes; optional ledger anchoring.
  • Privacy: DPDP Act alignment — minimisation, retention, erasure.
  • Resilience: rate limiting, WAF, secrets vault, backup/restore drills.

12.6 UI/UX guidelines

Map-first, mobile-first, and low-cognitive-load. Plain-language labels named for what citizens control. Every action keeps one verb through its flow. Empty and error states give direction, not apologies. Meets WCAG 2.1 AA with visible focus and reduced-motion support.

13 AI / ML & geospatial intelligence

From record-keeping to proactive governance

Satellite change detection

Spot unauthorised change

Compare time-series imagery to flag new construction, encroachment on public/forest land, or land-use change — with before/after tiles for officials.

Predictive analytics

Dispute & fraud risk

Score parcels for dispute likelihood from mutation frequency, ownership churn, and encumbrance patterns to prioritise review.

Workflow automation

Straight-through processing

Auto-route registration → mutation, auto-validate proposals against zoning, and surface exceptions for human decision.

Decision-support

Governance dashboards

Coverage, backlog, revenue, and data-quality KPIs; valuation hints from comparable transactions for planners and fiscal teams.

Responsible-AI stance: models assist, humans decide. Every AI flag is explainable, logged, and appealable; no automated adverse action on a citizen without official review.

17 Assumptions & open questions

Boundaries of this prototype

Assumptions

  • Mock/sample datasets stand in for live State systems in the demo.
  • ULPIN is available or can be minted for demo parcels.
  • Pilot contexts: one urban (Chandigarh), one rural (Tamil Nadu).

Out of scope (prototype)

  • Legally binding conclusive titling.
  • Production integrations with every State's live database.
  • Payment settlement beyond a simulated gateway.

Open questions

  • Definitive ULPIN format & minting authority per State.
  • Canonical CRS for each pilot State's cadastre.
  • Governance model for cross-State standards conformance.
